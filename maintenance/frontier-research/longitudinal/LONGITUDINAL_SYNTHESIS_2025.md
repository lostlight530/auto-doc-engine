## 3. Temporal turning points
1. **Q1 → Q2: reproducibility representation versus realized provenance.** A lock artifact standardizes a description of intended installations; subsequent composition/SBOM, document converter revisions, and packaging evidence answer different questions. Neither a lock nor a package establishes that a target environment was instantiated.
2. **Q2 → Q3: the evidence object gains typed relations and stable publication boundaries.** A compositional package is only a container of claims unless the software/source/role/review relations, released asset and AST transformation are separately identified. An immutable release can freeze a mistaken artifact just as effectively as a correct one.
3. **Q3 → Q4: source and attribution governance become part of artifact evidence.** Citation links, a source-management provenance track and additional parser/input formats extend the chain beyond output identity. They improve auditability while increasing the number of possible version, origin and transformation mismatches.

## 6. Annual synthesis
The 2025 narrative is not “more metadata makes research correct.” It is a decomposition of *which kind of evidence is missing at each boundary*. The first half of the year separates intention, installed environment, tool revision, and packaged composition; the second half separates typed attribution, publication mutability, source-review provenance, parser identity, and the derivative artifact. A provenance graph with more nodes permits narrower audits but can also reveal incompatibilities that one checksum would conceal. The category of a link—citation, source review, build, transformation, release—is as material as the presence of a link.

The research therefore supports **a method for asking stronger reproducibility questions**, not a verified improvement in scientific fidelity. Selected releases and specifications are authoritative for their own declared changes, not for this repository's runtime, a particular user's environment, or the truth of document contents.
